## Project Overview

Numerical Cognition in Mandarin-Speaking and English-Speaking 2-to-4-year-old children examines how language background, age, and number-knower level relate to performance across early numerical tasks in China and the U.S.

<div class="project-authors">Authors: Yuexin Li<sup>1</sup>, Langxu Jin<sup>1</sup>, Pangzhongyuan Pei<sup>1</sup>, Nina Lin<sup>1</sup>, Yintao Kang<sup>1</sup>, Luxin Zhang<sup>1</sup>, Huanyi Ye<sup>1</sup>, Wenjun Wu<sup>1</sup>, Jean Ee Tang<sup>1</sup>, Peter Gordon<sup>1</sup></div>
<div class="project-affiliations"><sup>1</sup> Teachers College, Columbia University</div>

## Background

- Children begin counting around age two, but exact set-size representation and cardinal-principle understanding develop over a longer period.
- Numerical cognition development may be shaped by both universal developmental factors and language/cultural context.
- The project tests development from subset-knower stages to cardinal-principle knower stages in Mandarin- and English-speaking children.

## Research Questions

- What role does language play in early numerical cognition development?
- Do Mandarin-speaking children become one-knowers later than English-speaking children?
- Do Mandarin- and English-speaking children show similar versus different patterns across verbal and non-verbal numerical tasks?

## Participants and Task Battery

- Participants:
  - `84` Mandarin-speaking children (mean age about `42` months; range `24–59` months)
  - `53` English-speaking children (mean age about `40.3` months; range `25–56` months)
- Performance was grouped by age and by number-knower status (from Given-N criteria).
- Tasks included:
  - Ascending and descending enumeration
  - Given-N task
  - Parallel and orthogonal one-to-one correspondence tasks
  - Nuts-in-a-can task
  - Rote counting task
  - Caregiver interview/context measures

## Analytic Plan

- Group and condition comparisons were tested across language, age-group, knower-level, and task context.
- Analyses emphasized language-group contrasts, age effects, and knower-level effects across task types.
- Statistical summaries included between-group comparisons and within-task trend analyses.

## Results Summary

- In both Mandarin and English groups, performance generally decreased as target set size increased across key tasks.
- Nuts-in-a-can performance was significantly stronger in the Mandarin group (`t = -3.03`, `p = 0.003`).
- In one-to-one correspondence tasks, the overall language-group difference was not significant (`t = -0.5`, `p = 0.62`), but English-speaking children showed a significant orthogonal-task difficulty pattern (`t = -2.23`, `p = 0.015`) not observed in the Mandarin group (`t = -1.02`, `p = 0.15`).
- Younger children and subset-knowers showed weaker ascending/descending task performance than older or higher-knower peers.

## Discussion

- Across most verbal and non-verbal tasks, Mandarin- and English-speaking children showed broadly similar developmental profiles, consistent with strong universal components in early numerical cognition.
- Results did not support a generalized delay account for Mandarin-speaking children in early number-word acquisition.
- Age emerged as a stronger predictor of performance than rote-counting skill or knower-level alone in several task contexts.
- Relying only on rote-counting performance may under-estimate the complexity of children’s numerical understanding.
